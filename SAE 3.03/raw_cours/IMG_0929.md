# IMG_0929 — Page 3: Séries chronologiques, définition et exemples

**Content:** Start of Section 1 ("SÉRIES CHRONOLOGIQUES"), subsection 1.1 "Définition et exemples".

- Formal definition of a **série chronologique** (time series) as a bidimensional statistical series indexed by time: t₁→x_t₁, t₂→x_t₂, …, t_n→x_t_n.
- Remarks: observations are ordered by time; data points are conventionally joined by line segments in plots.
- Boxed **"But de l'étude des séries"**: understand the past, predict the future, study links with other series.
- Boxed **"Méthodologie"** with a handwritten 5-step method: (1) graph the data, (2) choose a composition model (additive/multiplicative), (3) smooth via moving averages, (4) isolate/estimate trend and seasonal variations, (5) short-term prediction via reconstruction with 3 modeled components or exponential smoothing (student's choice).
- **Exemple 1**: "Airlines" dataset — Tableau 1, international air traffic in thousands of passengers per month, January 1949 to December 1960 (the classic AirPassengers dataset), source: go.to/forecasting.
